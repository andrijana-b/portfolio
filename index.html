Problem

Products were difficult to find, product pages lacked the information customers needed, and frequent checkout issues hurt the buying experience.   • Removed the old "Professionals" redirect, bringing B2C and B2B into one shopping experience
  • Worked with CRO to set a clear goal for each page type, so pages were designed with a purpose
  • Optimised PDPs with clearer USPs, trust signals, and reviews, and added expandable sections to help customers quickly find the product details they need
  • Designed to meet basic WCAG accessibility requirements and created consistent visual design

Tops

Recruitment · Storyblok · 2025

Problem

TOPS's recruitment website had cluttered navigation, competing CTAs with no clear primary action, and job listings that were easy to scan past. Weak visual hierarchy and no social proof made it hard for candidates to find and apply to vacatures, and the site's rigid structure made it difficult for the team to build new pages independently.

My Role

I ran a full UX audit of the existing site, then led a complete redesign of its structure and visual system in Storyblok, building a component library that gives the TOPS team the freedom to create and extend pages themselves as the site grows.

Key contributions

  • Ran a UX audit of the old website, identifying issues with visual hierarchy, competing CTAs, navigation, and job-listing scannability
  • Structured approach: split the work into three levels (Structure, System, Expression) so usability came first and brand styling was layered on top
  • Reusable component system: turned shared patterns into flexible components
  • Designed for Storyblok: gave the TOPS team a system for building new pages in Storyblok
  • One system, four sector identities: kept structure, spacing, typography and component behaviour the same across Groen, Agri, Infra and Bouw, while colour, labels and imagery change per sector
  • Vacancy experience: redesigned vacancy pages and tiles around what job seekers need to know quickly, with key facts up top and the apply button always visible

04

Sugarwish

Brainster UX/UI Academy · Gifting e-commerce · 2020

Problem

Sugarwish lets a gift buyer send a digital eCard with no mailing address required, and the recipient picks their own treats. But the existing ordering flow led users to give up when confronted with certain choices, usability testing showed the highest drop-off was on the "choose an eCard" and "choose a size" steps, and user interviews found some visitors suspected the site of being a scam because of an unclear sending flow.

My Role

As part of the UX/UI Academy at Brainster, I worked in a team of three to redesign a subset of screens on Sugarwish's existing website, without breaking the consistency of the live product. I led stakeholder and user interviews, usability testing, journey mapping, wireframing, and prototyping in Figma. Our solution was chosen by the company as the winning one among eight submitted.

Key contributions

  • Ran remote usability testing and user interviews to uncover pain points across the ordering flow
  • Introduced a progress indicator so users always know how many steps remain
  • Reordered and re-explained "The Select" product, the site's top seller, after testing showed users didn't understand what made it different
  • Redesigned the size-selection cards so pricing and flavour info sit above the fold without scrolling
  • Moved the eCard occasion filter to match users' natural eye movement, and limited the number of cards shown at once
  • Merged the login, send-eCard, and add-recipient steps into a single screen to remove an unexpected, unpopular login interruption
  • Added a cart item-count badge, a "Continue shopping" action, and moved login to the end of the flow, right before payment
