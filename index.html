Verstegen

Small Giants · Website · Design · 2025

10K+ Monthly revenue through the webshop

Problem

Verstegen's WooCommerce website no longer matched the needs of the business. Products were difficult to find, product pages lacked the information customers needed, and frequent checkout issues hurt the buying experience. B2B customers were sent to a separate website, creating an inconsistent brand experience, while limited analytics and poor accessibility made it difficult to optimise the experience.

My Role

As the UX/UI designer, I worked closely with the client lead, CRO specialist, and frontend developers throughout the project. We started with a client meeting to define the requirements for the new website. I redesigned the entire website, transforming the existing catalogue style website into a more user-friendly e-commerce Shopify platform.

Key contributions

  • Ran user interviews and card sorting to define how people expect products to be grouped, then redesign the category structure and navigation around that
  • Removed the old "Professionals" redirect, bringing B2C and B2B into one shopping experience
  • Worked with CRO to set a clear goal for each page type, so pages were designed with a purpose
  • Optimised PDPs with clearer USPs, trust signals, and reviews, and added expandable sections to help customers quickly find the product details they need
  • Designed to meet basic WCAG accessibility requirements and created consistent visual design
  • Handed off a fully specced Figma file with all assets ready for development

Problem

TOPS needed a user-friendly recruitment website that made it easier for candidates to find relevant jobs across four sectors: Green, Agriculture, Infrastructure, and Construction. The existing site was cluttered, with unclear navigation, competing CTAs, and job listings that were easy to miss.

04

Sugarwish

Brainster UX/UI Academy · Gifting e-commerce · 2022

Problem

Sugarwish lets a gift buyer send a digital eCard with no mailing address required, and the recipient picks their own treats. But the existing ordering flow led users to give up when confronted with certain choices, usability testing showed the highest drop-off was on the "choose an eCard" and "choose a size" steps, and user interviews found some visitors suspected the site of being a scam because of an unclear sending flow.

My Role

As part of the UX/UI Academy at Brainster, I worked in a team of three to redesign a subset of screens on Sugarwish's existing website, without breaking the consistency of the live product. I led stakeholder and user interviews, usability testing, journey mapping, wireframing, and prototyping in Figma. Our solution was chosen by the company as the winning one among eight submitted.

Key contributions

  • Conducted remote usability tests and user interviews to identify ordering-flow pain points.
  • Added a progress indicator to clarify remaining steps.
  • Repositioned and clarified the best-selling “The Select” product.
  • Redesigned size-selection cards on PDP to show pricing and flavours above the fold.
  • Improved eCard browsing with a reordered occasion filter and fewer cards per view.
  • Moved login to the final step before payment to reduce friction.
