01 · Outcome

One recruitment brand, four sector identities, and a block library that lets the website grow without a redesign

After handoff, the TOPS team built a whole new Professionals section and extra Vakschool course pages on their own, without a designer. They assembled them in Storyblok from the blocks I designed and our developers built.

That independence comes from a scalable UX/UI foundation:

One consistent TOPS experience on desktop and mobile

Four sector identities Groen, Agri, Infra and Bouw, on one shared system

A library of reusable blocks and patterns, ready to use in Storyblok

Designs for every key journey job seekers, career changers, employers and blog readers

02 · Context & The problem

One website, many audiences

TOPS is a Dutch recruitment and staffing company with four sector labels: Groen, Agri, Infra and Bouw. One website serves six audiences: job seekers, career changers and students looking at green-sector training, temporary workers, potential clients, existing partners, and people who want to work at TOPS itself. The company was in the middle of a brand refresh.

Job seekers landed on a homepage that spoke to everyone at once and had to dig for vacancies, and the TOPS team couldn't add a page without a designer starting from scratch.

My UX audit of the old site showed why candidates struggled:

01

No primary action

The homepage had many CTAs and none stood out. Two of them, "Bekijk alle vacatures" and "Vacatures", led to the same page.

02

Overwhelming navigation

Too many options, an oversized logo, and almost no feedback on hover or click.

03

Hidden vacancies

Job listings were buried in text. People scan before they read, so the listings were easy to miss entirely.

04

No social proof

There were no visible reviews to build trust.

Every page under a label needed that label's colour, while the site as a whole still had to read as one TOPS.

So the real question was: How do we build one recognisable brand
that the TOPS team can keep extending on their own?

03 · Key decisions

Three decisions that shaped the site

  1. 01

    Blocks, not pages

    I chose

    A library of reusable blocks (heroes, cards, vacancy tiles, search, forms)

    I rejected

    Designing 14 pages as one-off layouts, each restyled four times

    Because

    The TOPS team would build pages in Storyblok themselves. A fixed set of pages would be outdated the first time they needed a new one.

  2. 02

    One brand across four sectors

    I chose

    One system, four expressions: shared structure, sector colour and imagery on top

    I rejected

    Four separate sector designs

    Because

    Four designs would have felt like four unrelated sites and multiplied the work for design and development by four.

  3. 03

    Vacancies first

    I chose

    Vacancies as the main path: one primary CTA, a lister with filters, and a vacancy page that leads with the key facts

    I rejected

    Equal entry points for every audience, and job descriptions as long text

    Because

    Finding a job is why most people visit, yet the audit showed vacancies were the easiest thing to miss. Job seekers decide in seconds on location, hours and pay.

04 · System: blocks, not pages

Designing the building blocks, not the pages

Because pages would be assembled by the team, not by me, I stress-tested every block: short and long headings, different text lengths and image ratios, one or two buttons, three breakpoints, all four sector colours. I also checked how blocks sat next to each other, so any combination still produced a clear page.

05 · Expression: one system, four sectors

Groen, Agri, Infra and Bouw from one set of parts

A big part of the work was taking one component and making it work for Groen, Agri, Infra and Bouw (Green, Agriculture, Infrastructure and Construction).

Structure, spacing, typography, buttons and interactions stay identical everywhere; only background colours, labels and imagery change per sector.

The sector colours weren't decoration. They became part of the navigation: a quiet, constant signal of which part of TOPS you're in.

06 · Structure: vacancies first

Where a visitor becomes an applicant

For job seekers, vacancies became the one clear primary action:

Homepage A search in the hero, sector tiles showing how many jobs each label has open, and a permanent "Alle vacatures" button in the header. The duplicate CTA went.

Lister page Filters for location and postcode, working hours and distance.

Vacancy page Answers the seven questions a job seeker asks up front (what, where, hours, pay, experience, tasks, how to apply), with Apply visible the whole way down.

Social proof The homepage also shows what the old site lacked. Workers placed by TOPS rate it 8+, and a strip of client logos shows who TOPS works with.

Navigation The old header packed an oversized logo, a "Bel ons direct" dropdown, five menu items, social icons and two equal-weight buttons (Inschrijven and Vacatures) into one bar. The new one groups the site by audience under Werkzoekenden, Opdrachtgevers and TOPS Vakschool, with the four labels underneath each. Over TOPS, Werken bij, Blogs, FAQ and Contact moved to a separate top bar, and one button stays primary: Alle vacatures.

07 · What didn't make it to launch

The job alert

I designed a job alert for the lister page, so job seekers didn't have to keep coming back to search. But the vacancies live in TOPS's HelloFlex system, not in the website, so alerts needed an extra integration. With a fixed go-live date, we prioritised the core job search and application flow, and the alert was cut.

08 · What I learned

Consistency doesn't mean making everything look identical

The project pushed me beyond individual screens, to see how brand, information architecture, components, sector identities, user journeys and conversion connect. The real work was balancing three forces:

Consistency

Users recognise one TOPS everywhere and understand how it works.

Differentiation

Each sector has its own identity and feels relevant to its audience.

Scalability

The design works across many pages without adding complexity for designers and developers.

The most valuable thing I designed wasn't a page. It was the rules behind the pages: enough structure to keep the experience consistent, and enough freedom for TOPS to grow.
